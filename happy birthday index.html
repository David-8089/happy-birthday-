💌 A Little Note, From the Heart

Happy birthday, love. Before anything else — before the wishes, before the food, before whatever else today brings you — I need you to sit with this letter for a moment, because there are things I should have said out loud a long time ago, and I'm done waiting for the "right time."

Do you remember how we started? How something so simple turned into something neither of us saw coming? I think about that a lot lately — how two people who didn't even share the same space could build something that felt like home anyway. Our night calls when the whole world was asleep and it was just your voice and mine. The way we laughed at nothing, over nothing, for hours, and it never once felt like nothing.

I miss our arguments too — the lovely ones, the ones that weren't really about winning, just about two people who cared enough to fight for understanding instead of walking away. We had rules, remember? Rules we made not to control each other, but to protect what we had.

I got so wrapped up in school and everything else pulling at me that I let the small things slide — the calls that used to be automatic, the little check-ins, the effort that told you that you mattered more than my to-do list. I didn't cheat, I didn't lie, but I think that's almost harder to explain, because it wasn't one moment — it was months of you feeling like you came second to things that shouldn't have outweighed you. I get it now. Being loved isn't just about not doing anything wrong — it's about showing up, consistently, even when life gets full. These past months of silence taught me that. I don't want to just say I'll do better — I want you to actually feel the difference, starting now.

Mercy, I know these past months of "normal talk" haven't been normal at all — not for me. I've felt the distance every single day. I'm not writing this to pressure you or to rush what you need time to think about. I'm writing it because it's your birthday, and I couldn't imagine another year passing without you knowing, clearly and without doubt — I still love you. Not the memory of you. You, now, exactly as you are.

We used to say we loved expensively — not carelessly, not cheaply, but like something worth protecting. I still believe that. I still want to build that with you, if you'll let me.

Happy birthday, Mercy. Whatever you decide, know that this love was never something I said lightly.

Yours,
David
